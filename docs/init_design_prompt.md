## Context
Vital Fascia is evolving from a massage/soft-tissue practice into an education and authority brand around fascia, movement, body awareness, posture, pain, recovery and long-term physical health. The site has two jobs:
1. Now: convert visitors into 1:1 treatment bookings.
2. Soon: act as the funnel into Alex's upcoming 8-week 1:1 in-person therapy program. Design the offer section so this program can slot in later without a redesign.

Core philosophy to communicate through structure and design: Alex doesn't want clients dependent on treatment. He helps people understand their bodies, recognise their patterns, and leave with tools to look after themselves outside the treatment room.

## Copy
Use placeholder/filler text throughout. Alex will write the final sales copy. Keep headings short and realistic in length so the layout reflects real content, but don't spend effort on wording. Use real section labels so the structure is clear.

## Audience
Health-conscious adults, roughly 25–45: desk workers, professionals, gym-goers, runners, active people, and people with recurring issues who feel they've tried lots of things without understanding what's actually going on.

## Emotional journey
The page should move the visitor through: Curiosity → Recognition → Trust → Possibility → Action.
The takeaway we want: "This guy looks at the body differently. This makes sense. I want to learn more or work with him."

## Page structure (single scrolling page, in this order)
1. Header / navigation: logo wordmark "Vital Fascia", anchor links to sections, persistent "Book now" button. Sticky on scroll.
2. Hero: strong, clear message about what Alex does and who he helps. Large editorial photography placeholder. Primary CTA (Book a session) and secondary CTA (e.g. learn about the approach / free resource).
3. "Does this sound like you?": recognition section covering recurring pain, tension, stiffness, poor posture, restricted movement, and the frustration of trying everything without answers.
4. What I help with: common issues/areas. Also reference his modalities: remedial massage, myofascial release, trigger point therapy, myofascial cupping, dry needling, corrective exercise.
5. The approach: explain how it differs from just treating the painful area (whole-body, fascia-led, pattern-focused thinking). This is the "intelligence" moment of the page; consider a simple diagram or visual device that conveys connection across the body.
6. The process: five clear steps: Understand, Assess, Treat, Move, Empower. Make this feel like a journey, not a bullet list.
7. Current 1:1 offer: session details, what's included, price placeholder, booking CTA. Structure it so a second card/panel for the 8-week program can be added later (optionally show it as a "coming soon" teaser).
8. Testimonials / social proof: a few client quotes with name and context (e.g. "Runner, 34").
9. About Alex / my story: personal, human, warm. Portrait photography placeholder.
10. Lead magnet / email capture: free resource download (currently a functional breathing guide) plus signup to his newsletter, "The Fascia Flow". Name + email fields.
11. Final booking CTA: calm but confident closing section.
12. Contact / location: Sydney CBD address placeholder, map placeholder, email, Instagram (@thefasciaguy), hours placeholder. Simple footer.

## Credibility metrics
Weave a few statistics/metrics through the page to build credibility, e.g. sessions delivered, years in practice, average client rating, % of clients reporting improvement. Use placeholder numbers. Keep it light: 2–3 touchpoints across the page, not a stats-heavy wall. Alex is early in his practice, so these should feel honest and understated, not boastful.

## Design direction
Keywords: grounded, masculine, organic, human, premium, minimal, earthy, intelligent, calm, strong, warm, modern, embodied.

The sweet spot is modern clinical intelligence expressed through a basic, human understanding of movement. Editorial rather than clinical.

- Palette: warm neutrals: bone, sand, stone, deep olive, charcoal. Olive or charcoal as the anchor/accent; avoid bright or saturated colours.
- Typography: clean and considered. A refined serif or characterful display face for headings paired with a clean sans-serif for body works well for the editorial feel. Generous type sizes and line heights.
- Layout: lots of whitespace, strong grid, asymmetric editorial compositions where they help, confident section rhythm with alternating light and dark bands.
- Photography: large, strong, natural-light images of real bodies in movement, hands-on treatment, texture and form. Use tasteful placeholders sized and cropped as real photography would be.
- Motion: subtle and calm (gentle fades/reveals on scroll). Nothing flashy.
- Fully responsive; must work beautifully on mobile, since most visitors will arrive from Instagram.

## Avoid
The site must not feel like: a hospital or medical clinic, a day spa, a generic physio clinic, a bodybuilding/fitness site, a hyper-spiritual wellness brand, or a hard-sell internet funnel. No stock-photo clichés (lotus poses, hot stones, candles, anatomical skeleton charts), no neon or clinical blues, no countdown timers or aggressive pop-ups.
